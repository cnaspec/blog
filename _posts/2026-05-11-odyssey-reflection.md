# Service to the World Odyssey 

Although I am not eligible for Odyssey credits (and don't fully understand what they are), I have attempted to reflect on the work done during the course in relation to the requirements for an "Service to the World" Odyssey credit.

## What skills and talents are most valuable in carrying out the social change work you are doing with your community partner? How well do those skills mesh with the talents or limitations you have come to see in yourself through the experience?

I suppose that the technical skills are obviously important, as they would be necessary to create the project that the client wants us to do (if they could do it themselves they would, as that would be preferable to entrusting it to some random college students). Beyond that, time managemment and organisational skills are very important since we have a strict timeframe with deadlines that we need to meet. Teamwork and communication skills are also necessary (in part due to this) because it needs to be very clear what the clients want us to create, and what separate team members are working on so that the time can be utilised effectively and there is no duplication of effort.

I think that the project has made clear to me that whilst the course was really comprehensive, actual projects can be complicated and there is always something else that you need to learn or another technical skill that needs to be developed. It can also be difficult to communicate with team members when everyone has different schedules. Teamwork adds an extra dimension of complexity as you are no longer working on your own terms. 

I think it is easier to face challenges when you enjoy what you are working on, and from this experience I have learnt that I do not particularly enjoy web development or working with databases. This is definitely a limitation for me, but all it means is that I have to find something I enjoy doing, so the project was defintely a useful experience.


## Consider the social, environmental, or spiritual need on which you are focusing for this Odyssey project. Do you feel that the approaches to creating social change employed by your community partner are effective ways of creating change in this area? Why or why not?

I do think that the approaches employed by our community partner with regards to the Seed Library project are effective for encouraging more people to take up garderning. I had to recently help one of my friends in the Nature Club plant some new bushes over by the apartments and it was a lot of work (especially weeding the garden bed). I think if I was interested in starting gardening the resources and programs the library offers would be really helpful in helping me know where to start, as I was completely clueless and my friend had to direct all of us in what to do. Additionally, the Seed Library itself helps to remove the finiancial barrier into starting gardening, and the seeds being free might lead to people considering trying it out who might not have otherwise thought about it due to the combined time-effort cost of starting out.

I also think that having an online version of the Seed Library which allows patrons to view available seeds will be really helpful towards their goals, as it will allow people see what seeds are available without having to make a trip to the library (again encouraging people who are vaguely interested in gardening but might not have started otherwise). I don't know what the process is for checking out seeds from one of the affliated libraries that FCL posts seeds to is, but it might make the process simpler if the patrons can view whats available online and so won't ask for things that FCL doesn't have. If this is the case, it reduces the burden on the library staff and frees up time for the other outreach programs the library does.

Overall, I think the online seed library database (once fully completed) will help the FCL to encourage people to start garderning.

## Have any of your fundamental values, social ideals, or significant beliefs been challenged or confirmed through this experience? Have you found any of your academic coursework helpful in understanding what you have observed and experienced? Anything you learned in class that you would directly challenge now that you have had this experience?

I don't think that any of my fundamental values or social ideas have been challenged by this project. Gardening is not a particularly controversial subject, and I feel that is pretty well known that you should try to support your local library as they often provide important services to the community beyond simply lending books! Anything that gets people invested in nature and in preserving ecosystems is a good thing, and for children gardening can be a good way to get them invested in considering where their food comes from.

However, I did think it was interesting when the guest speaker came in to talk to us about website development, and discussed making sure that the websites comply with accessibility requirements. This wasn't something I had particularly though about before, and whilst I knew it was important to accommodate others I hadn't thought about it in the context of web development. I did make me realise how easy it is to overlook that people might have different needs, which is something I need to be more careful about considering going forward.

## There are many social, political, environmental, or community issues on which you might work in the years ahead. How has this experience reshaped your views on the public issues that call you to action?

As mentioned above, whilst I don't think this work has reshaped my views on public issues that called me to action as none of my values have changed significantly, learning about the accessibilty problems websites can have will probably change my approach on tackling community issues. It is entirely possible something that you think is good or inconsequental may cause issues for somebody else, and so it is important to take everyone's views into account when implementing change. 

I might however look into gardening when I go home because I have been reading about the problems a lack of plant diversity is causing insects (important for pollination!). Since we have a few garden beds at the back of the house which nothing has been done with for a while it might be a good idea to use them for something useful.
